This report presents a clear and well-structured analysis of medication supply disruption risk at MetroCare Health System and provides a strong justification for prioritizing this issue as the organization’s most significant operational risk. The prioritization framework is particularly effective, using a weighted scoring model across patient safety impact, financial exposure, operational disruption, likelihood, and reputational risk to transparently demonstrate why medication supply instability represents the highest-priority risk.

The mitigation strategy is well designed and organized into two complementary initiatives: improving system-wide inventory visibility and standardization, and implementing structured demand forecasting and shortage alerting. These initiatives directly address the root causes of the risk, including limited inventory visibility, reactive procurement processes, and weak forecasting capabilities. Importantly, the strategy is intentionally designed to work within MetroCare’s operational constraints by leveraging existing ERP reporting tools rather than requiring major system replacement.

The analytics integration section clearly identifies the operational metrics needed to support decision-making, including stockout rates, forecast variance, vendor fill rates, and emergency procurement costs. The proposal also includes threshold-based alerts and a structured reporting framework that ensures analytics outputs directly inform operational decisions.

The value creation section effectively connects operational improvements to measurable financial and risk outcomes, including reductions in emergency procurement spending, lower stockout rates, and improved forecasting accuracy. The implementation roadmap further strengthens the proposal by outlining a realistic sequence of actions during the first 90 days and the following year.

Overall, this report demonstrates strong analytical reasoning, practical operational planning, and clear executive-level communication.
